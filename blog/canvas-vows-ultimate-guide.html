What Are Canvas Vows?

Canvas vows are your actual wedding vows printed on premium canvas material and stretched over a wooden frame, just like fine art. Unlike a simple paper print, a canvas vow art piece is designed to last a lifetime without fading, tearing, or needing glass.

A personalized wedding vow canvas typically includes:

  • Your names
  • Your wedding date
  • The full text or an excerpt of your vows
  • Elegant typography and design

Companies like Canvas Vows specialize in turning these words into museum-quality decor. The result is not just a decoration—it is a daily reminder of the commitment you made.

Types of Canvas Vows Art You Can Create

1. The Classic Full Vow Canvas

This traditional option features the complete text of both partners' vows in elegant, readable fonts. It is perfect for couples who wrote heartfelt, detailed vows and want every word preserved.

Best for: Romantic, sentimental couples who want the full story.

2. The His and Hers Vow Set

One of the most popular options is a two-canvas set: one canvas with his vows and one with hers, designed to hang side by side. This creates a balanced gallery-wall look.

Best for: Modern homes, above the master bed, or in the living room.

3. The First Look or Shared Vow Canvas

If you and your partner wrote the same vows or want to feature one meaningful set of promises, a single-canvas design is clean and powerful. It often features your names at the top and date at the bottom.

4. The Minimalist Typography Canvas

For couples who love modern design, this style uses large serif fonts for names and delicate script for headings such as “I Vow.” A clean body font keeps the piece readable from a distance.

5. The Watercolor and Floral Canvas Vows

Many personalized canvas vows incorporate watercolor backgrounds, floral illustrations, or colors from your wedding bouquet to tie the art to your wedding-day aesthetic.

How to Design the Perfect Canvas Vows

1. Choose Your Words Wisely

You don't have to include every pause or repetition. Edit your vows down to the most powerful 150–250 words. Keep the promises, personal stories, and “I love you because...” moments. If your vows were very long, consider using an excerpt and preserving the full version in a keepsake book.

2. Typography Matters

The best canvas vows use a combination of two or three fonts:

  • A bold serif or sans-serif for names, such as Playfair Display or Montserrat
  • An elegant script for headings such as “I Vow”
  • A clean, readable serif for the body text, such as Garamond or Lora

Avoid using an overly curly script for the entire vow because it can become difficult to read.

4. Select Your Canvas Finish

Look for a 1.25-inch-thick museum-wrapped canvas with archival inks. This gives it a frameless, high-end look and helps protect the art from fading. A matte finish is best for readability and reduces glare.
